\section{Materials \& Methods}

A small group of healthy volunteers (n=10) will be scanned with the chest protocol established in the LIFE study with a repeatability approach. For each sequence type (Cartesian, radial, SMS), each subject will undergo multiple types of respiration to explore the potentially clinically significant differences in PWV from different types of respiration. This includes free-breathing, breath-holds at inspiration and expiration, and the Valsalva maneuver, which has significant physiological effects on intrathoracic pressure and heart rate37. In the case of the Cartesian sequences, only the breath-holds will be performed as motion artifacts will make the images unusable. For the research sequences, the free-breathing acquisitions will be reconstructed at both expiration and inspiration, while the breath-hold acquisitions will be reconstructed using approximately half of the acquired projections (in order to match the number of projections used for the free-breathing acquisitions with 50\% thresholds). All other reconstruction parameters will match the standard LIFE reconstructions. PWV will be calculated for each type of breathing and sequence and for both sessions using the GUI described previously using just one PWV method (TTF) for simplicity.
PWVs will be compared between each type of respiration to analyze the differences between each. Differences in expiration and inspiration for the free-breathing and breath-hold sequences will be compared to see if the difference is consistent or if breath-holds have an effect. Results will be compared between the test and retest portions to analyze the reproducibility.

\section{Results}
Global aortic PWV values were calculated for radial 2D PC data in 90 subjects from the LIFE study reconstructed at inspiration and expiration. The mean aortic PWV at inspiration (7.22m/s, SD=3.30m/s) was 13.7\% lower (p=0.007, CI=[0.29, 1.84]) than the mean aortic PWV at expiration (8.28m/s, SD=4.10) (Figure 4). Under free-breathing conditions, MRI-based PWV measurements show a significant decrease in aortic PWV between expiration and inspiration. This is consistent with results from a pressure tonometry study in the carotid-radial artery which also found a significant decrease in PWV from expiration to inspiration38.
